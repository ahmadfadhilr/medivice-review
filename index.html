Before You Ask

The Questions Sponsors Ask First

If your question is not here, ask it. We would rather answer it before you commit to anything.

Do you work on pharmaceuticals as well as devices?

No. Clinical investigation and clinical evaluation of medical devices is our entire business. Our SOPs, templates and monitoring approach are built for ISO 14155 — not adapted from pharma.

Can we engage you for a single document rather than a whole study?

Yes. Beyond full service, we take functional engagements — monitoring, data management or medical writing only — and advisory work covering strategy, gap analysis, feasibility and second opinions. Retained support for ongoing CER and PMCF maintenance across a portfolio is also available.

Will we deal with the people who actually scoped the study?

Yes. Whatever the shape of the engagement, one named project manager owns it end to end, and the people who scope your study are the people who run it. Senior involvement is not a pitch-stage promise, and escalations reach a decision-maker the same day.

Is your Indonesian execution subcontracted to a local partner?

No. Site qualification, ethics committee and Kemenkes submissions, hospital and investigator contracting, investigational device import and customs documentation, in-country monitoring and site training are carried out by our own team. Those things cannot be run remotely. We are here, and we do them ourselves.

Which markets can one evidence package support?

We work routinely across Indonesia (Kemenkes and Permenkes), ASEAN (AMDD and CSDT), the EU (MDR 2017/745), Singapore (HSA), Malaysia (MDA), Thailand, the Philippines, Vietnam, and China (NMPA bridging). Studies run in Indonesia and Southeast Asia are structured so the dataset can support submissions elsewhere — a decision made at protocol and eCRF design stage, not at reporting stage.

Do you train our in-house team, or only run the work for us?

Both. We train investigators and site teams, study coordinators and research nurses, sponsor clinical and regulatory staff, ethics committee members, and manufacturers building a first in-house clinical function. Sessions are workshop-based and use real documents rather than slides alone, in English or Bahasa Indonesia.
